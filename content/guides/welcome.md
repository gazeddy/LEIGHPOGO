## So you want to know more about Max Battles!

That shundo Charizard is no good here unless it is also a **Max Pokémon**. Only Dynamax and Gigantamax Pokémon can enter Max Battles.

It is not all bad news: Max Pokémon can still be used normally in raids, Gyms and Trainer Battles. Eligible Max Pokémon can also Mega Evolve, but they cannot enter a Max Battle while the Mega Evolution is active.

> **Limits and costs can change.** Event bonuses may temporarily alter the normal limits, and the upgrade screen is always the final word for a particular Pokémon.

## Getting started

Your first Max Pokémon normally comes from the **To the Max!** Special Research or your first visit to a Power Spot. A Max Battle party contains up to three Pokémon, and standard Max Battles allow up to four Trainers.

Max Battles work differently from ordinary raids:

- Max Particles are deducted only after a win. Losing lets you change the team and retry without paying again.
- There is no normal raid-style countdown, but the boss eventually becomes desperate and starts dealing enormous damage.
- Attacking fills the Max Meter. When it is full, your active Pokémon Dynamaxes or Gigantamaxes for three Max turns.
- If all three Pokémon faint in a three-star battle or higher, use **Cheer** to help the remaining Trainers fill their Max Meters.

### Difficulty

- **One-star:** usually an easy solo with a sensible Max Pokémon.
- **Three-star:** often soloable, but counters and investment matter.
- **Five-star:** expect prepared counters, roles and teamwork.
- **Six-star Gigantamax:** the hardest battles. These can contain up to 20 Trainers, coordinated in smaller teams.

A large lobby does not guarantee a win. A smaller prepared group can outperform a larger group with poor counters and no support moves.

## Max Particles and Power Spots

Max Particles, or **MP**, are used to enter Max Battles and train Max Moves. Collect them at Power Spots and while exploring.

Under normal conditions:

- You can collect up to **800 MP per day**.
- You can carry up to **1,500 MP**.
- Your first interaction with a Power Spot can award extra particles.
- Special events may temporarily raise these limits.

Plan your collection before Max Monday or a community battle session. Max Monday runs from **6:00 p.m. to 7:00 p.m. local time**, so starting with plenty of MP lets you battle, collect more and repeat.

After a win, you can station one of your battle-party Pokémon at the Power Spot. It helps later Trainers through an attack bonus and can earn Candy before returning to you. Recall it when you need it or when the Power Spot closes.

## Powering up a Max Pokémon

Max Pokémon are expensive because normal levels and Max Moves are upgraded separately. Fully maxing everything is not necessary.

Choose a useful species with good attacks and defensive typing. Better IVs are welcome, but the correct species, moves and role matter much more than the small difference between good and perfect IVs.

### Recommended upgrade order

1. **Evolve it to its final useful form.**
2. **Power it to around level 40.** Level 50 helps in the hardest battles but is not the first priority.
3. **Train its attacking Max Move.**
4. **Unlock Max Guard or Max Spirit when it has a support role.**
5. **Raise moves further or go to level 50 only when the battle or long-term value justifies the cost.**

Candy, Candy XL and MP costs vary by species. Legendary and higher-tier builds can consume hundreds of Candy and Candy XL plus thousands of MP, so check the complete cost before committing.

> **Trading warning:** trading a Max Pokémon resets its trained Max Move levels. Trade first, then invest.

## Team roles

Difficult Max Battles are easier when the group agrees roles before entering.

### Attacker

The cheapest initial role. Concentrate on:

- A Fast Attack that produces the Max Move type required for the boss.
- Super-effective damage.
- Training the attacking Max Move.

During the Max phase, attack unless the group urgently needs support.

### Tank

A tank needs bulk, resistance to the boss's attacks and a trained **Max Guard**. Use Guard during the Max phase to reduce incoming damage and keep the team alive for another cycle.

### Healer

A healer needs enough bulk to survive and a trained **Max Spirit**. During the Max phase, check the team health bars: heal when needed and attack when everybody is healthy.

### Suggested team of four

A strong starting arrangement is:

- Two attackers
- One tank
- One healer

Training all three Max Moves gives flexibility, but it is expensive. Build the role the group needs first.

## Battling

### Normal phase

The aim is to fill the Max Meter without losing the team.

- Keep attacking to build the meter.
- Short Fast Attacks are often useful because they can be repeated quickly.
- Use Charged Attacks when they improve the matchup rather than pressing them automatically.
- Swipe into the energy bubbles on the battlefield for extra Max Meter progress.
- Dodge when it will keep an important tank, healer or attacker alive.

### Max phase

Each Trainer receives three Max turns:

- **Attacker:** use the attacking Max Move.
- **Tank:** use Max Guard.
- **Healer:** use Max Spirit when healing is needed; otherwise attack.

Coordinate before difficult battles. Four people all assuming somebody else brought healing usually ends badly.

If your Pokémon faint, keep using Cheer. In a larger Gigantamax battle, your Cheers continue helping the surviving Trainers. A failed attempt does not consume the entry particles, so regroup and retry before the Power Spot closes.

## Picking teams

For each boss, consider both attack and defence:

- Attackers need super-effective Max Moves and strong damage.
- Tanks need bulk and resistance to the boss's possible attacks.
- Healers need to survive reliably; raw attack is secondary.

Useful all-rounders save resources because they cover several matchups. Pokémon such as **Excadrill**, **Metagross** and **Greedent** have all had useful Max Battle roles, but none is universally best.

Before investing, ask:

1. Is it useful against more than one boss?
2. Does its Fast Attack produce the Max Move type I need?
3. Can it fill an attacker, tank or healer role?
4. Can I afford the important levels and moves?

Three accessible, properly trained counters are often better than one perfect counter and two unprepared fillers.
